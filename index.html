01 · Crypto basics

What is a digital asset?

A digital representation of value recorded on a cryptographically secured distributed ledger — e.g., a blockchain.

Cf. I.R.C. § 6045(g)(3)(D). No physical form: owning crypto means controlling its private key.

Hosted (custodial)

A third party — an exchange like eToro or Coinbase — holds the keys for you. Functions like a brokerage account.

Unhosted (self-custody)

You hold the keys yourself — cash under the mattress. Lose the seed phrase and the coins are gone for good; no one can reset it.

Why it matters here: Paschall used a hosted wallet — which is how eToro could restrict his transfers. How many stakers use hosted wallets and how many use their own is unknown (more on that later).

01 · Crypto basics

What is a blockchain?

A decentralized "distributed ledger" in which transactions are recorded and confirmed by many independent computers ("nodes") instead of one central authority.

  • Public / permissionless: anyone can run a node — Bitcoin, Ethereum, Cardano
  • Private / permissioned: an operator decides who validates — e.g., enterprise or bank settlement ledgers
  • Built to record crypto transfers, but can also record ownership of traditional assets like stocks and bonds
01 · Crypto basics

The value proposition: consensus

Unlike traditional ledgers policed by a central authority, blockchain "offers greater transparency as to ownership by being based on consensus as to the accuracy of the transactions consummated on the network."
"[B]ecause adding, changing, or removing information from the blockchain is made purposefully difficult," it is "harder to falsify a transaction or hack into the ledger itself."

Friel v. Dapper Labs, Inc., 657 F. Supp. 3d 422, 426–27 (S.D.N.Y. 2023).

Takeaway: security comes from making cheating expensive. Validators are paid to bear that cost — and that payment is what Paschall taxes.

01 · Crypto basics

Two ways to reach consensus

"Mining" · Bitcoin

Proof of Work

nonce 000000 → hash 0000…
  • Miners race to solve a computational puzzle to add the next block; other nodes verify → token reward
  • Cost of cheating: wasted electricity and hardware
"Staking" · Ethereum (since 2022), Cardano

Proof of Stake

tokens locked as collateral
  • Validators are selected algorithmically, weighted by stake (and sometimes tenure or chance) → token reward
  • Uses ~99.9% less energy than proof of work
  • Cost of cheating: capital — penalties or forfeited stake ("slashing") on many networks. Pooling lets small holders join in.

Both run on public blockchains. The real difference is what the validator puts at risk: energy or capital.

02 · Pre-Paschall baseline

How the law stood before Paschall

2014

I.R.S. Notice 2014-21

Virtual currency is property, which may be a capital asset. Mined coins are gross income at FMV on receipt.

2021–22

Jarrett v. United States (M.D. Tenn.)

A Tezos staker sues for a refund, arguing rewards are self-created property. The IRS pays the refund; the case is dismissed as moot. No ruling.

July 2023

Rev. Rul. 2023-14

Staking rewards are income in the year the staker gains "dominion and control," valued at FMV on that date.

2023

Kim v. Commissioner

T.C. Memo. 2023-91: cryptocurrency is treated as property for federal income tax purposes.

Open question: the IRS took a position, but no court had tested it. Would taxing staking rewards on receipt hold up?

03 · Paschall — facts

The facts

  • Paschall held Cardano (ADA) on eToro, a hosted platform. Cardano uses proof of stake.
  • eToro staked users' tokens by default. Users could opt out, but then received no rewards.
  • eToro passed 75–90% of rewards to users, depending on membership tier, and kept the rest.
  • Paschall's tokens were staked all of 2021, earning monthly rewards automatically. Sounds like a dividend?
03 · Paschall — facts

2021: always de-risk, folks!

Cardano went from ~$0.18 to a $3.10 peak to ~$1.31 in one year (approximate prices). Each monthly reward is taxed at its value on the day it was received, even if the price later falls.

03 · Paschall — facts

Facts, continued

Nov 2021

eToro delists Cardano

Citing a shifting regulatory environment, eToro restricts transfers of ADA to outside wallets and platforms. But Paschall could still sell for cash, and chose not to.

2022

Restrictions lifted

Paschall moves his tokens off-platform.

IRS position

All 2021 rewards = 2021 income

Each monthly reward is taxable when received.

The issue: were staking rewards income on receipt? And did the transfer restriction defeat "dominion and control" in 2021?

03 · Paschall — posture

Procedural posture

Paschall v. Comm'r, T.C. Memo. 2026-46 — a memorandum opinion, which the Tax Court describes this way:

"Generally, a Memorandum Opinion is issued in a regular case that does not involve a novel legal issue. A Memorandum Opinion addresses cases where the law is settled or factually driven."

U.S. Tax Court, Guidance for Petitioners: Things That Occur After Trial (emphasis added).

  • This was a question of first impression, yet it came out as a memo.
  • Burden of proof didn't matter: the rewards were 2021 income even if the Commissioner bore the burden.
  • The Court reasoned from first principles, not Rev. Rul. 2023-14. Revenue rulings never bound the court, and after Loper Bright courts are even less likely to defer.
  • The Court clearly wanted expert economic analysis. It was silent on Jarrett. Opportunistic enforcement?
03 · Paschall — holding

A ruling from whole cloth: staking rewards are income when received

1 · Breadth

Presume Congress meant "to use the full measure of its taxing power."

James v. United States, 366 U.S. 213, 218–19 (1961)

2 · Accession

Income = "undeniable accessions to wealth, clearly realized, and over which the taxpayers have complete dominion."

Comm'r v. Glenshaw Glass Co., 348 U.S. 426, 431 (1955)

3 · Control

"The power to dispose of income is the equivalent of ownership of it."

Helvering v. Horst, 311 U.S. 112, 118 (1940)

Applied: Paschall could convert his rewards to cash at any time, even during the transfer restriction. So he had dominion and control in 2021.

04 · Critiques

Distinguishing Eisner v. Macomber

Monthly tokens look like a stock dividend, and Macomber held that a stock dividend is not a realization event:

The dividend "did not alter the pre-existing proportionate interest of any stockholder or increase the intrinsic value of his holding."

Eisner v. Macomber, 252 U.S. 189 (1920). Think of the pizza: more slices, same pie.

  • The Court: rewards go only to stakers, so each staker gains real value relative to non-stakers. That is a shift in proportionate interest, which Macomber lacked.
  • But: distinguishing Macomber doesn't show that realization occurred. The reasoning sounds more like the dissents: Holmes (the ordinary meaning of "income") and Brandeis (economic substance over form).

04 · Critiques

Self-created property? Rejected.

🎂 📖 🌾
Taxpayer

Staking tokens are "like a baker's cake or a writer's book — products of labor and capital that yield income only upon sale."

⚖️
Court

"Their analogy is misplaced. Stakers do not create anything . . . The fact that these tokens may be newly created is immaterial because the stakers are not the ones who created them."

The carve-out: "petitioners were not owners or operators of a staking pool; unlike the baker or writer, they lacked the power to decide whether (and when) the property was created."

So owners and operators of staking pools, who do control whether and when tokens are created, may still have a self-created property argument. Individual stakers don't.

04 · Critiques

A gap in the record: who actually stakes?

We don't know how much staking happens through hosted platforms (eToro, Coinbase) and how much happens from self-custody wallets. On Cardano, for example, holders can delegate directly from their own wallets. The opinion doesn't address it, and the outcome may depend on it.

The Macomber analogy

The Court's distinction depends on stakers gaining at non-stakers' expense. If nearly everyone stakes (platforms opt users in by default, and self-custody delegation is easy), rewards are close to pro rata. Then it looks a lot more like a stock dividend.

Dominion and control

Hosted stakers depend on the platform: eToro could, and did, restrict transfers. Self-custody stakers control their own keys. Paschall's hosted facts were arguably the weakest case for control, so the holding likely reaches further.

Who gets taxed in practice

Platforms report rewards to the IRS; self-custody stakers mostly self-report. Self-custody stakers who run their own pool may also fit the operator carve-out. Similar stakers could end up with very different results.

04 · Critiques

Doctrinally tidy, internally inconsistent, and blind to how crypto works

The Court contradicts itself

  • Stock or cash? To distinguish Macomber, tokens are treated like shares (a proportionate interest). To apply Glenshaw Glass and Horst, they are treated like cash. They can't be both.
  • Zero-sum or windfall? The Macomber distinction rests on value shifting from non-stakers to stakers. Yet the Court taxes the full reward at FMV and ignores the dilution that offsets part of it.
  • Who creates? Stakers "do not create anything," yet pool operators who decide "whether (and when)" tokens are created might. Staking is either creation or it isn't.

How crypto actually works

  • Cash vs. coin: the Glenshaw Glass taxpayer received dollars. Paschall received more of the same coin.
  • Holders stay in crypto. The Court relied on Paschall's ability to cash out, but staying in crypto is the whole point. A theoretical power to sell is a poor proxy for realized gain.
  • Volatility: a reward valued at about $3 in September was worth about $1.31 by December. Tax is owed on value the staker may never see.
  • Staking is network security that every holder benefits from. It's not a passive return.
04 · Critiques

An alternative framing: dilution, not income

New tokens increase supply, which dilutes every holder. Much of a staking reward just keeps the staker even; the people who actually lose are the ones who opted out. Staking is the price of decentralized verification.

Split the reward in two

① Pro-rata share

The portion that offsets dilution and compensates for the risk of backing the network, proportional to tokens staked. Treat it like capital: no gain yet.

② Transfer from opt-outs

Value shifted from non-stakers to stakers: a true accession to wealth. Treat it as ordinary income.

A second line of attack: the Court treats this as zero-sum, like issuing new stock. But more tokens aren't necessarily worth more to the holder. Price, liquidity, and lockups all matter.

05 · Implications

Implications

Inconsistent treatment

The "who created it?" test may treat miners and pool operators differently from passive stakers. Yet all of them bear cost and risk, and all are essential to the network.

A loophole for the platforms

The pool-operator carve-out lets exchanges like eToro claim deferral while individual stakers pay tax on receipt. A legislative fix has been proposed: the Tax Clarity for Mining and Staking Act (Rep. Carey).

Why people stake

Many blockchains require fees in their native token, and for many holders (privacy, libertarian values) the point is to stay on-chain. Staking is about the coin, not the platform.

Supply and value

Supply caps vary: Bitcoin at 21M coins, Cardano at 45B ADA. Prices are driven largely by speculation, but validation itself secures the network and may raise the value of every token.

05 · Implications

Open questions and room for good lawyering

Ambiguities

  • Jurisdiction: where does staking income arise when validation happens everywhere at once? Where do the stakers live?
  • Choose your analogy: harvesting crops (self-created property) or notarizing documents (services income)?
  • Pools: when exactly does a staker become an "owner or operator"?

Opportunities

  • Thin precedent: Jarrett was mooted, the revenue ruling gets no deference, and Paschall is only a memo. There's room for novel arguments, and the Court wants expert analysis.
  • Loss harvesting: rewards are taxed at FMV on receipt, and the wash-sale rule (§ 1091) covers stock and securities, not crypto. If prices fall, sell, book the loss, and buy back.
  • Low-bracket and de minimis stakers: compliance burden vs. revenue.
